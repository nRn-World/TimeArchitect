# Time Architect — Visual Direction

## World

A welcoming, dreamlike archipelago of clockwork puzzle chambers. The illustrated start screen
introduces an ivory-stone world in a twilight sky. In play, the canvas depicts each chamber as a
floating, top-down board, so the layout and the direction of movement are immediately legible.

## Shape and colour

- Rounded ivory tiles are walkable; blue-grey raised blocks are walls.
- Dark blue tiles are the void. Closed bridges and gates are marked with a cross; open ones have
  mint planks. A dashed gold outline connects the selected clock to its controlled tiles.
- The Architect wears an ivory cloak and a mint scarf. Anchors are glowing gold crystals.
- Brass clocks control time; coral mechanical eyes mark drones. Each object also has a distinct
  shape so that gameplay does not depend on colour alone.
- Green dots indicate available adjacent moves, while an exclamation mark warns of danger on the
  next tick. Optional dashed ghosts show upcoming drone positions and closing tiles.

## Composition and motion

The whole grid is visible, with reserved space for the HUD and controls. The camera adapts to
portrait and landscape layouts. Motion is restrained: an eased step, softly glowing anchor and
small rings for actions. Reduced-motion mode removes animation and easing. Five chamber palettes
change the atmosphere without altering the meaning of controls or hazards.

## Interface

The objective and run stats stay at the top, with movement and time controls separated at the
bottom (or at the sides in landscape). A scrollable object strip shows each mechanism's current
and next phase. Text labels, keyboard focus, hint controls, larger targets and a high-contrast
setting remain available alongside touch, keyboard and gamepad input.
